# If you think you've been scammed

**Act fast. The sooner you call, the better the chance of stopping the money.**

1. **Call your bank or card company first**, on the number on the back of your card. Ask them to stop or reverse the payment.
2. **Contact whoever you paid through** (below).
3. **Change passwords** for any account the scammer may have seen, starting with email. Turn on two-step sign-in.
4. **Report it** (below). Reports help stop the same people reaching others.

## If you paid

Per the FTC's [what to do if you were scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed). Refunds are not guaranteed.

| You paid by | Contact right away |
|---|---|
| Credit or debit card | The card issuer. Ask for a refund. |
| [Gift card](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/avoiding-and-reporting-gift-card-scams) | The card's issuer, on the number on the card. Say a scammer tricked you. Keep the card and receipt. |
| Wire transfer | The wire company and your bank. Ask for a recall. |
| Zelle or a bank transfer | Your bank or credit union. Ask them to reverse it. |
| Payment app (Venmo, Cash App, PayPal) | The app. Ask them to reverse it. |
| Cryptocurrency | The exchange or crypto ATM operator. Recovery is hard, so move fast. |
| Cash sent by mail | U.S. Postal Inspection Service: **1-877-876-2455**. |

## Where to report (United States)

| What happened | Where |
|---|---|
| Any scam or fraud | FTC: [**ReportFraud.ftc.gov**](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) |
| Someone used your identity, opened accounts, or filed taxes as you | [**IdentityTheft.gov**](https://www.identitytheft.gov/), which gives you a recovery plan |
| Online fraud, business email compromise, or investment and crypto scams | FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center: [**ic3.gov**](https://www.ic3.gov/). File whatever the amount, with full payment details. Reports can help the FBI freeze stolen funds, and the FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report says to ask your bank for a recall right away. |
| Fraud against an older adult | [National Elder Fraud Hotline](https://ovc.ojp.gov/program/elder-fraud-abuse/national-elder-fraud-hotline), Justice Department: **1-833-FRAUD-11** (1-833-372-8311), weekdays 10am to 6pm Eastern time |
| A scam text | Forward it to **7726** (SPAM), then delete it ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-report-spam-text-messages)) |
| A threat or immediate danger | Local police, or **911** |

## Protect your credit

If personal details were shared, [freeze your credit](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-credit-freezes-and-fraud-alerts) with **Equifax**, **Experian**, and **TransUnion**. It stops new credit being opened in your name. It's free and doesn't affect your score.

## People who can help

[AARP Fraud Watch Network Helpline](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/helpline.html), free: **877-908-3360**, weekdays 8am to 8pm Eastern time. Or someone you trust.
