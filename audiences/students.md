## Pace and setup

- 45 to 50 minutes, to fit a class period. Students use their phones in the lab.
- For high school groups, invite the school counselor in advance, especially if you cover sextortion.

## Which scams to show

[Job and task scams](../guide/job-scams.md), [marketplace scams](../guide/marketplace.md), [scam texts](../guide/scam-texts.md), and [code theft](../guide/code-theft.md), framed around gaming and social media accounts ("I sent you my code by accident, can you send it back?").

## What to emphasize

- **Your accounts are worth money.** Gaming, social media, and email accounts get stolen and sold. Two-step sign-in protects them.
- **Never send money or codes to get money.** Any job, prize, or deal where you pay first is a [scam](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams).
- **Sextortion.** Scammers pose as someone your age, trade photos, then threaten to share them unless paid. Keep this calm and factual, and say clearly it's not their fault. Paying rarely stops it. Get help first: block the account, keep the messages, and tell a trusted adult. NCMEC has [sextortion guidance](https://www.missingkids.org/sextortion), and [Take It Down](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/) can help remove nude or explicit images of minors from participating platforms.
- **Telling someone isn't snitching.** Name who students can go to.

## Lab twist

Have students also check their most-used app for two-step sign-in, active sessions, and linked devices.
