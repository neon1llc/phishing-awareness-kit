title: Facilitator script
description: "What to say at each slide, the questions to ask, and how to handle the moments that come up in a scam awareness session."
---

Slide numbers match [slides.md](slides.md). Say the quoted words in your own voice. Others in the room have spotted more scams than you, so ask for their stories.

## Welcome (slides 1 to 3, 5 minutes)

**Slide 2.** Take the show of hands. "These messages go to everyone. Getting one isn't your fault." Invite one or two short stories. If someone lost money, thank them and move on.

**Slide 3.** "By the end, everyone will have two-step sign-in on for their email. Keep your phone nearby."

## The four pressures (slides 4 to 6, 7 minutes)

**Slide 5.** Ask for an example of each pressure. "Most scams use these, not all. Some are friendly for weeks before money comes up."

**Slide 6.** Repeat all session: "One pressure, slow down. Two, stop and check."

## The scams people see (slides 7 to 11, 10 minutes)

Pick stories that fit your group (see [audiences](../audiences/index.md)). Ask "What's the tell?" before revealing it.

- **Slide 7, texts.** Open the company's app or type its address yourself.
- **Slide 8, calls.** A real fraud team never needs a code you received. It's how they sign in as you.
- **Slide 9, emergencies.** Hang up and call the person on the number you already have, or another family member. The code word comes at the end.
- **Slide 10, invoices.** Workplace groups. Any change to bank details gets a call to a number already on file, even for the boss.
- **Slide 11, the long game.** If someone you've never met in person raises money, talk to someone you trust first.

## Spot the scam (slide 12, 10 minutes)

Hand out the [card sets](../exercises/spot-the-scam.md) and walk between tables. Then go through four or five cards with the room, asking a different table each time. Praise reasoning over right answers. "Can't tell yet" is fine with a way to check.

## The pause (slides 13 to 14, 8 minutes)

**Slide 13.** "If the call was real, the company will still be there."

**Slide 14.** Run one [role-play](../exercises/role-plays.md) in front of the room, playing the scammer. Push a little: "If you hang up, the charge goes through." Cheer when they hang up anyway.

## Lock the front door (slides 15 to 16, 5 minutes)

**Slide 15.** Mention password managers briefly. The one built into your phone or browser is a fine start.

**Slide 16.** "A sign-in code is a key someone mails you. If anyone asks you to read it out, they're trying to use it."

## Two-step sign-in lab (slide 17, 10 minutes)

Follow the [lab guide](../exercises/two-step-lab.md). Pair quick finishers with people who are stuck, and offer a follow-up to anyone who can't finish.

## Who to tell, and the takeaway (slides 18 to 20, 5 minutes)

**Slide 18.** Fill in the workplace reporting line beforehand. For families, suggest a code word tonight, in person, never by text. Hand out the [who-to-call cards](../handouts/who-to-call.md).

**Slide 19.** Hand out the [one-page takeaway](../handouts/one-page-takeaway.md). "Put it by the phone."

**Slide 20.** Take questions. Collect the [feedback form](../handouts/feedback-form.md) if you use it.

## Moments that come up

- **"I already lost money."** Tell them to call their bank today, and point to the [who-to-call card](../handouts/who-to-call.md).
- **"I'm not good with technology."** "The pause needs none."
- **"Isn't two-step sign-in a hassle?"** "Most services only ask on a new device. It takes seconds."
- **A question you can't answer.** "I'll find out and send it with the takeaway." Then do it.
