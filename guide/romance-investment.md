---
title: Romance and investment scams
description: "A new online friend or partner who, after weeks of kindness, introduces an investment, a crypto platform, or an emergency that needs money."
---

## What it looks like

It starts with a "wrong number" text, a dating app match, or a friendly social media message. Weeks of warm conversation follow. Then the turn: a crypto platform where they've made a lot, a small investment that seems to grow, and encouragement to add more. Or an emergency: a medical bill, a customs fee, a ticket to finally visit. When you try to withdraw, there are fees, taxes, or a freeze. The money is gone.

## The tell

- Someone you've **never met in person**, who always has a reason not to video call, starts talking about money.
- An investment with guaranteed or unusually high returns.
- Pressure to keep it from family and friends, "who wouldn't understand."

## What to do

Talk to someone you trust before sending money. Search the platform's name with the word "scam." Never move money to a platform or wallet someone online pointed you to.

## If it already happened

Stop sending money, even for "fees to release your funds." That's the same scam's second stage. Report it at [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/). Anyone who later offers to recover the money for a fee is a known follow-up scam.
