---
title: Two-step sign-in lab
description: "A guided hands-on activity: everyone in the room turns on two-step sign-in for their email and checks their recovery details."
---

**Time:** 10 to 15 minutes. **Goal:** everyone leaves with two-step sign-in on for their main email and current recovery details.

## Before the session

Ask people to bring their phone and know their email password. Check guest Wi-Fi works. For groups over fifteen or older adults, bring a helper for every eight people.

## Steps to put on screen

Menu names change. If one doesn't match, search settings for "2-step," "two-step," or "two-factor."

- **Gmail (Google).** Open myaccount.google.com, choose **Security**, then **2-Step Verification**. A passkey or phone prompt is fine.
- **Outlook, Hotmail, or Live (Microsoft).** Open account.microsoft.com, choose **Security**, then **Manage how I sign in** or **Advanced security options**, and turn on **Two-step verification**.
- **iCloud (Apple).** On an iPhone, open **Settings**, tap your name, then **Sign-In & Security**. Two-factor is usually already on. Check your trusted phone number.
- **Yahoo.** Open **Account security** and turn on **Two-step verification**.
- **Work email.** If it isn't on, tell whoever runs IT.

## Which second step

Any beats none. Strongest first:

1. **A passkey or security key.** Resists phishing: it won't work on a fake sign-in page.
2. **An authenticator app or phone prompt.**
3. **A text message code.** Fine to start with.

## Recovery

Check the recovery phone number and email. If they're out of date, you may not get the account back. Save backup codes in a password manager or on a printed sheet at home.

## If someone gets stuck

- **Forgot their password:** start recovery now, or note their name and follow up.
- **No smartphone:** text message codes work on any mobile phone. Some services offer voice calls or printed backup codes.
- **Worried about lockout:** show them the backup codes and recovery options. The code is only asked for on a new device.

## Close

Ask everyone who turned it on to raise a hand, and say the count.
