title: Spot the scam
description: "Twelve printable message cards, some scams and some real, with an answer key naming each pressure and how to check."
---

**Time:** 10 minutes at tables, then 5 together. **Setup:** print a set per table and cut the cards apart. Swap in two or three real messages your group has received, with names, numbers, and links removed. Keep the answer key.

**Task:** sort the cards into *scam*, *real*, and *can't tell yet*. Name the pressure on each scam, and say how you'd check each *can't tell yet*. All links and numbers here are fake.

## The cards

**Card 1, text message.**
> USPS: Your package is on hold due to an incomplete address. Update within 12 hours: usps-redelivery-help.example.com

**Card 2, email.**
> Your monthly statement is ready. Sign in to the app or at the address on the back of your card. We'll never ask for your password or a code by email.

**Card 3, phone call (read aloud).**
> "This is Dana from your bank's fraud department. We've blocked a $900 charge at an electronics store. To confirm it's you, I'm sending a code to your phone. Can you read it back?"

**Card 4, text message.**
> Hi Mom, I dropped my phone in the toilet lol. This is my new number. Text me back here? Need a favor

**Card 5, pop-up on a computer screen.**
> WARNING! Your computer has 5 viruses. Do not close this window. Call Microsoft Support at 1-555-0148.

**Card 6, email to a bookkeeper.**
> From: accounts@supplier-co.example.com
> Our bank has changed. Please use the attached details for the outstanding invoice and all future payments. We need this settled today to avoid a hold on your orders.

**Card 7, text message.**
> Your verification code is 482913. Don't share this code with anyone. We'll never call to ask for it.

**Card 8, social media message.**
> I made $14,000 last month with this crypto platform. My mentor can show you too. Limited spots, message me today!

**Card 9, email.**
> Your password was changed on Tuesday at 10:42 PM. If this was you, ignore this email. If not, secure your account from the app or by visiting the website directly.

**Card 10, phone call (read aloud).**
> "This is Officer Reyes with the county sheriff's office. You missed jury duty and there's a warrant for your arrest. Resolve it today with gift cards. Don't hang up, or we'll send a deputy."

**Card 11, email at work.**
> From: the CEO (personal email)
> Are you at your desk? Pick up some gift cards for a client today. Keep this quiet, it's a surprise. I'll reimburse you.

**Card 12, text message.**
> Reminder: your dentist appointment is tomorrow at 2:30 PM. Reply C to confirm or call the office to reschedule.

## Answer key

| Card | Answer | Pressures | How to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scam | Hurry | USPS texts only people who asked for tracking, with no links. Track on the official site. |
| 2 | Real (probably) | None | It points to the app or card, not a link. |
| 3 | Scam | Fear, hurry | A code request means they're signing in as you. Hang up and call the number on your card. |
| 4 | Scam (very likely) | Secrecy (a new number), soon hurry | Call your child's old number, or another family member. |
| 5 | Scam | Fear, hurry | Real companies don't put phone numbers in pop-ups. Force quit the browser. |
| 6 | Scam | Hurry, odd payment (new account) | Call the supplier on a number already on file. Never change bank details from an email. |
| 7 | Real | None | A code you asked for. If you didn't ask, someone has your password: change it. |
| 8 | Scam | Hurry, odd payment (crypto) | Friend accounts get hacked. Call your friend. |
| 9 | Can't tell yet | Fear | Could be either. Don't click. Open the app or type the site's address and check your security settings. |
| 10 | Scam | Fear, hurry, odd payment (gift cards) | Courts and police never take gift cards. Hang up and call the court's number from its website. |
| 11 | Scam | Hurry, secrecy, odd payment (gift cards) | Reach the CEO on a number you already had. Report the email. |
| 12 | Real (probably) | None | No link. If unsure, call the office on a number you already have. |

## Takeaway

Real messages send you somewhere you already know: the app, your card, your usual sign-in. Scams send you somewhere new: their link, number, or payment method. Each type, with sources, is in the [field guide](../guide/index.md).
